M8 investigation presentation amendment — v0.2, 21 October 2026

The detailed v0.1 field catalog already specifies renal chemistry and urine studies.
This amendment makes their bedside visibility explicit without claiming that new
laboratory feeds, entry contracts or calculations have been implemented.

Core visible fields: urea/BUN as identified by the source, creatinine, serum/plasma
sodium and potassium, and urine routine. A urea value is not a BUN value; preserve
analyte and original units. No automatic cross-conversion or clinical interpretation.

Expandable electrolyte, acid–base and CRRT monitoring: chloride; chemistry total
CO2; blood-gas bicarbonate and pH, separately identified; total and systemic ionised
calcium; circuit post-filter ionised calcium separately; magnesium; phosphate;
glucose and albumin. Keep actual specimen/location, collection/report times, quality
and haemolysis flags. Direct versus indirect sodium assay, interference/dilution,
diuretics, replacement and actual KRT context are important source metadata for
future interpretation. A critical-result workflow requires local thresholds and
acknowledgement, not a new automatic treatment rule in this view.

Expandable urine studies: urine routine/dipstick components; microscopy with cells,
casts, crystals and source morphology; ACR and PCR as distinct ratios; urine sodium,
potassium, chloride, creatinine and urea; urine osmolality and measured serum
osmolality; specific gravity. Routine report components may also include pH,
ketones, colour and appearance when supplied by the laboratory. Do not use a urine
output/collection entry as a urinalysis result. Culture is available when infection
is a clinical question, with original specimen and M6 ownership retained.

Measured osmolality uses mOsm/kg. Estimated osmolarity uses a separately specified
calculation/basis and must not be silently relabelled as a measurement. Specific
gravity does not replace measured osmolality. Paired blood/urine collection times,
sampling before/after relevant interventions, KRT, diuretics, sepsis and CKD context
must remain visible where interpretation depends on them. Urine sodium, FENa and
FEurea do not generate an automatic volume-status or cause-of-AKI diagnosis.

Targeted options: cystatin C, CK/rhabdomyolysis evaluation, immune/glomerular tests,
haemolysis/TMA evaluation, medication/transplant drug levels, renal imaging and
obstruction review. These are indication-led options, not a universal order bundle.
ACR/PCR, urine electrolyte calculations, osmolality/gap interpretation and sodium
correction calculations retain their v0.1 eligibility and approval requirements.

Current implementation: five core fields and expandable groups. Only currently
supported exact source discriminants (creatinine and relevant blood-gas observations)
open source/history. Other options explicitly read 'Result feed not connected'.
No result supplied, retrieval unconfirmed and feed not connected remain different
states. No invented observations, test ordering, source writes or expanded laboratory
admission were added. The source-linked four-card renal workflow is unchanged.

Verification for this amendment: 41 tests passed across the new investigation panel,
existing renal bedside and shared M7 workspace tests; typecheck/production RF2 build
passed. Existing large shared schema bundle advisory remains. No backend contracts
or clinical rules were changed; the earlier backend/clinical validation boundaries
remain in force. See outputs/clinrx-m8-investigations-20261021 for current evidence.

Clinical anchors:
NICE NG148, recommendation1.4.2, urinalysis when AKI is suspected/detected:
https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng148/chapter/Recommendations
NICE NG203, proteinuria assessment and ACR/PCR distinctions:
https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng203/chapter/Recommendations
European hyponatraemia guideline (ESE/ESICM/ERA-EDTA,2014):
https://www.ese-hormones.org/publications/directory/ese-clinical-guideline-for-the-management-of-hyponatraemia/
These support specific investigation concepts; the UI organization is a ClinRx
design choice, not a claim that every listed test is required for every patient.
