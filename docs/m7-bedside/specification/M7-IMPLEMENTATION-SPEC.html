1. Bedside simplicity is a requirement

Keep one primary review screen with the four cards above. Immediately below it show a short Review next area and one intervention/response timeline. The main view contains current clinically relevant information, not the full field dictionary. Reuse existing recorded data; request only a missing input that changes the current assessment or chosen action.

Always retain patient/encounter identity, review time, affected source freshness and the responsible team. Put the actual measurement time and method beside values. Use plain states: “Below documented target,” “Pressure unreliable,” “Perfusion concern,” “Not assessed,” “Source delayed” and “Specialist review needed.” Do not display “Stable,” “Covered,” or “Safe” based on one number, an empty feed or a completed software review.

  • Default view: relevant BP/MAP, HR/rhythm, documented pressure target, concise perfusion summary, actual vasoactive delivery, phenotype/uncertainty, and the next owned review. Show a lactate trend only if available and comparable; do not require lactate to open the screen.
  • Progressive disclosure: select a card to inspect its source evidence, contradictions and prerequisites. Advanced CO/PAC/echo details, fluid tests, concentration arithmetic and specialist/device context open only for the clinical question. One normal number cannot hide an outstanding safety concern.
  • Minimal entry: typically confirm assessment/target, select the relevant next review and record rationale/owner when missing. Do not ask users to manually enter hundreds of integration fields. An incomplete form never blocks the institution's urgent bedside response.
  • Action labels: “Confirm measurement,” “Review perfusion,” “Assess fluid benefit and harm,” “Review vasoactive delivery,” “Escalate to senior/shock team,” and “Record plan.” Only locally enabled options appear. The screen does not remotely start, stop or titrate treatment.
  • Timeline: actual administrations and measured responses at their real times, with gaps and confounding events. Ordered/planned interventions have a distinct style and cannot look delivered. No causal claim from a before/after picture alone.
  • Interaction: keyboard and screen-reader access, text with every color state, no color-only urgency, visible source age, appropriate decimal precision, safe loading/error/empty states, no hidden treatment defaults. Preserve selection during ordinary refresh; stale clinical proposals require explicit review.

The information inventory is broad because the software must handle uncertainty and safety accurately. It is a backend contract and disclosure model, not a bedside form or an instruction to order every possible investigation.

3. Clinical scope and ownership

The starting intended use is clinician-directed adult hospital/ICU hemodynamic review. Scope includes reliable pressure and flow assessment, organ-perfusion review, shock phenotype and trajectory, fluid benefit/harm review, vasoactive preparation/delivery safety, response reassessment and escalation. An evidence card can remain available while an unsupported calculation or treatment prompt is withheld.

M7 consumes exact dependency inputs from M6 infection assessment, M8 kidney physiology/support, M9 fluid balance, M10 respiratory/ABG, M11 bleeding/transfusion, M12 devices/procedures, M13 sedation, M16 neurology and relevant other context. It does not duplicate their ledgers, order sets or management algorithms. This export contains only M7 requirements. Existing code comments assigning imaging to a historical roadmap number are implementation history; current module ownership must follow the current ClinRx roadmap.

Pregnancy, pediatric/neonatal care, major burns, complex congenital/shunt physiology, neurocritical CPP targets, post-arrest care, transplantation and mechanical support require explicitly validated specialty profiles. Source evidence remains reviewable, but generic adult-sepsis thresholds and drug profiles cannot silently apply. MCS context is a proposed read-only specialist interface; device management and settings are not implemented or authorized here.

4. Evidence changes the design, not just the bibliography

Clinical recommendations, expert good-practice statements, neutral trials and engineering controls have different authority. The source register states what was actually read. This was a targeted primary-source review, not an exhaustive systematic review. Full protocol tables, commercial device capabilities and every specialty pathway have not been curated into executable rules.

  • Monitoring: ESICM2025 supports integrated perfusion, applicable dynamic testing, echo and expanded monitoring in persistent shock. Its arterial-catheter guidance and SSC2026's qualified invasive/noninvasive BP recommendation are not silently merged; the local profile must specify the population and exact adopted statement. ESICM circulatory shock and hemodynamic monitoring guideline, 2025; Surviving Sepsis Campaign adult guideline, 2026.
  • Sepsis reference: SSC2026 gives an initial MAP of65mmHg and a conditional60–65mmHg range for adults65years or older. Initial-fluid guidance remains conditional and individualized, with frequent reassessment. These are reference statements requiring local adoption and patient-specific targets, not universal software prescriptions. Surviving Sepsis Campaign adult guideline, 2026.
  • Perfusion: ANDROMEDA-SHOCK did not meet its primary mortality comparison. ANDROMEDA-SHOCK-2 improved a hierarchical outcome, chiefly through vital-support duration. Model its multimodal phenotype logic and uncertainty; do not advertise isolated CRT as a proven mortality-saving control signal. ANDROMEDA-SHOCK, 2019; ANDROMEDA-SHOCK-2, 2025.
  • Fluids: FRESH supports studying flow-based PLR-guided decisions, with trial size and platform limits. CLOVERS, CLASSIC and the later ARISE FLUIDS strategy trial do not establish a universal fluid-first or pressor-first winner. ARISE FLUIDS was published after SSC2026; the adoption record must retain that chronology. FRESH fluid response evaluation trial, 2020; CLOVERS, 2023; CLASSIC, 2022; ARISE FLUIDS, 2026.
  • Vasoactives: SOAPII's arrhythmia findings and DOREMI's neutral primary comparison support explicit agent/phenotype tradeoffs rather than interchangeable drug logic. Exact agent, formulation, concentration, route and dosing units require local profiles. SOAP II, 2010; DOREMI, 2021.
  • Cardiogenic support: SCAI stage is one part of risk assessment. ACC guidance supports structured team-based evaluation. ECLS-SHOCK and DanGer-Shock studied different devices and selected populations, with materially different benefit/harm results. No stage or score can independently select a device. ACC concise cardiogenic shock clinical guidance, 2025; SCAI shock-stage consensus update, 2022; ECLS-SHOCK, 2023; DanGer-Shock, 2024.
  • Fluid products and targets: SMART, PLUS and BaSICS have different designs and endpoints. SEPSISPAM and the65trial do not justify one pressure range for all patients. Preserve individual goals and source-specific limitations. SMART, 2018; PLUS, 2022; BaSICS, 2021; SEPSISPAM, 2014; 65 trial, 2020.

5. Required clinical decision sequence

  1. Recognize the concern: hypotension and hypoperfusion are separate clinical findings. Preserve the institution's immediate emergency pathway; incomplete software data cannot delay bedside assessment.
  2. Trust the inputs: confirm patient assignment, method/site/compartment, acquisition time, quality and applicable calibration/leveling/zeroing. A cuff does not require arterial-transducer zeroing. Do not average conflicting sources to manufacture certainty.
  3. Understand the patient: current targets, baseline, cause, bleeding/infection, LV/RV/valves, rhythm, ventilation, medication exposure, access and relevant support. Allow mixed or undifferentiated shock.
  4. Assess perfusion: bedside skin/CRT, mental state, interval urine context, lactate and oxygen/flow findings where available. Consider confounders and discordance. Elevated lactate is not fluid deficiency; normal MAP or lactate is not absence of shock.
  5. Consider an intervention: for fluids, ask need, potential response and tolerance separately; test only when applicable and clinically appropriate. For vasoactives, assess phenotype/target, actual preparation/delivery and adverse effects under the approved agent profile.
  6. Reassess: document actual intervention, pressure/flow/perfusion response, congestion/harm, confounding changes, next clinical action and owner. Do not imply causation from temporal sequence.
  7. Escalate or de-escalate deliberately: persistent deterioration requires cause/measurement/access review and timely senior/specialist response. Removal/weaning needs supported individualized criteria and ongoing perfusion assessment.

Each branch returns a structured state: met, not met, not evaluable, not applicable or invalidated. Missing input never becomes false/normal. Unsupported clinical advice is withheld with a specific reason and ownership; existing raw source-critical communication remains independently available.

6. Fluid responsiveness is a method, not a fluid order

PLR records the actual maneuver, stable baseline, rapid supported CO/SV or VTI response, protocol-defined interval, reversibility and competing changes. A pressure rise alone is not automatically a flow response. Pain, position constraints, intra-abdominal pressure and measurement sensitivity can limit inference; inability to perform a test is not a negative test.

PPV/SVV require their exact method's rhythm, spontaneous-effort, ventilation/VT-PBW, chest/mechanics, RV, abdominal and device conditions. No single cutoff works across all devices and conditions. End-expiratory-occlusion, mini-fluid and tidal-volume challenges require trained clinician performance and validated protocols; M7 never changes ventilator settings. Separate source reported interpretation from ClinRx-derived interpretation.

Static CVP, PAWP, IVC or volumetric preload indices alone do not determine fluid responsiveness or fluid need. Congestion assessment integrates oxygenation/lung, LV/RV, venous findings, balance and actual response; no isolated congestion score proves global fluid tolerance. A positive flow response with pulmonary edema can still favor withholding further fluid after clinical review.

7. Vasoactive safety and actual delivery

Curate each locally supported agent and formulation separately, beginning with norepinephrine, epinephrine, vasopressin, phenylephrine, dopamine, dobutamine and milrinone if in scope. Other pressors, inodilators/vasodilators, rescue agents and adjuncts require their own authorized profiles. Antiarrhythmic, steroid or other specialty therapy is a linked clinical decision, not an inferred vasoactive variant.

For every product specify active base/salt/activity convention, concentration/diluent/stability, accepted dose units and weight method, route/access, population/indication, contraindications, monitoring, preparation checks, prescriber/nursing authority, titration limits, escalation and weaning. This package deliberately supplies no unapproved dosing table. Where sources give insufficient guidance, an honest unknown remains disabled rather than a plausible-looking default.

Orders, preparation, initiation, ongoing delivery, partial delivery, pauses, restarts, boluses, bag changes and stopping remain distinct. Record the actual concentration/rate/dosing weight and line/pump channel at each segment. Pump activity alone does not prove delivery into a patent vein. Bag dead-space/carrier/changeover risks require explicit local protocols; no automatic flush, rate compensation or antidote order is allowed.

Peripheral use must preserve urgency while enforcing the actual local site/patency/inspection/escalation policy. SSC2026 does not provide universal peripheral dose, duration or location limits; midlines were not covered by that recommendation. Extravasation/ischemia concerns route to immediate owned bedside assessment even when an exact numeric dose is missing.

8. Numerical requirements and limitations

Every calculation requires an active approved profile with exact input types, units, method/time alignment, denominator/physiologic constraints, constants, uncertainty and rounding. A formula listed here is a design reference, not an activated bedside calculator. Preserve measured versus calculated versus hypothetical results and the full unrounded value.

Do not silently replace source units, reinterpret saturation percent as fraction, mix CO/CI or pulmonary/systemic compartments, substitute ScvO2 for SvO2, or use a stale/unaligned CO with current MAP. Negative pressure observations can be real; denominator guards and physiological validity are method specific. All source unknowns, censored results and unsupported ranges remain explicit.

9. Real-time consistency and failure behavior

  1. Authenticate source, bound raw intake and retain original payload before semantic interpretation.
  2. Resolve exact patient/encounter/device assignment. Identity-incomplete material stays operationally owned; do not fabricate a patient record to preserve a critical message.
  3. Normalize with a versioned adapter, preserving source identities, units, clocks/lexemes, comparators and quality. Reject structural corruption safely; retain appropriate original evidence.
  4. Append immutable source revision and committed admission receipt idempotently. Source release, receipt, commit and clinical awareness are different events.
  5. Build bounded, rebuildable views at a declared cutoff. No interpolation, forward-filled drug rate or missing-as-zero physiology. Reverse membership is a projection; canonical source objects do not hash one another recursively.
  6. Evaluate scoped approved profiles with exact inputs and coverage. Watch new relevant facts and absence assumptions as well as old pin changes. Recheck context transactionally before plan acceptance and any separately authorized host-system handoff.
  7. Persist explicit decisions/receipts and notification outbox work atomically where required. Consumers need deduplication and durable ownership; distributed exactly-once effects are not assumed.
  8. Track revision/attempt-specific communication through delivery, acknowledgment, review and actual action. Corrected/withdrawn critical content creates a new duty.
  9. During outages expose affected scope and use existing bedside monitor/lab/escalation pathways. Backfill is idempotent and clearly historical; restored heartbeat alone does not establish clinical completeness.

Numeric source-specific freshness/latency/SLOs, averaging windows, resource caps and clinical acknowledgment deadlines must be fixed by P02/P03/P11 for the actual intended use. Monitor acquisition-to-release/receipt/commit/display/dispatch/delivery/acknowledgment separately. Do not invent one universal latency number.

For writes, durably admit original command, request digest and fenced worker generation before effects. A timeout or empty lookup cannot prove no effect. Recover exact retained results without recomputation; unknown external effect remains unknown unless downstream guarantees and authoritative reconciliation establish it. Terminal no-effect needs a durable fence preventing delayed old work and proof about external effects. No automatic resubmission or changed UUID solves uncertainty.

10. Safety acceptance and human factors

The hazard register below is a planning inventory. Local severity, likelihood, residual risk, owner and acceptance are still required. No document can certify all possible future clinical conditions.

  • Verify source adapters with actual permitted/de-identified examples: waveform artifacts, device swaps, clock loss, source gaps, late charting, preliminary/corrected reports, orphan/foreign bindings and pump/MAR disagreements.
  • Test clinically meaningful combinations, not only single fields: target-achieved BP with poor perfusion; high lactate with adrenergic/metabolic causes; responsive but congested; AF with PPV; high-IAP/unsafe PLR; RV/PH/obstructive causes; asynchronous echo/flow; concentration/weight changes; transfusion/bleeding context; support-related measurement failure.
  • Independent clinical reviewers must approve exact expected outputs and dangerous-error definitions before running validation. Formula boundary/units/rounding tests require independent numeric oracles per profile. The broader scenario outlines here are not completed runnable oracles.
  • Conduct prospective non-interventional shadow evaluation, adjudicating missed and false alerts, unsupported advice, delay, subgroup effects and clinician interpretation. Lock intended use and acceptance criteria before results are observed.
  • Human-factors tasks must show that nurses and clinicians distinguish ordered versus given, reliable versus suspect, target pressure versus perfusion, tentative phenotype versus diagnosis, reference versus patient plan, hypothetical versus chart value, and acknowledgment versus action.
  • Measure whether users can identify the urgent issue, open the relevant source and record a next review efficiently. Set and approve task-completion, error and burden criteria during design validation; do not claim a universal click/time target equals safety.
  • Subgroups include age, phenotype, skin/peripheral assessment limitations, organ support, rhythm, method/device/software, source completeness, clinical setting and supported specialty contexts. Average performance cannot conceal an unsafe subgroup.
  • Inject failures at commit/response, projection, outbox, roster, network, restore and reconciliation boundaries. Verify access privacy, exact retained history and unresolved-command continuity across refresh/reload/device change.

All critical predefined scenarios must pass with no unresolved critical defects, but no universal sample size, PPV threshold or dose tolerance is invented. Local clinical owners must set prespecified acceptance and stop criteria. If a feature cannot meet its intended use, retain supported evidence/review access and keep the dependent advice disabled.

Checked 21 October 2026. Primary abstracts, society recommendation pages and selected publications were inspected as stated. Full specialty/device/protocol curation and a systematic review have not been completed. The proposed field/rule/workflow design includes independent engineering requirements; no cited paper certifies the resulting software.

  1. S01 · ESICM circulatory shock and hemodynamic monitoring guideline, 2025. Adult critically ill; primary abstract and official society material checked. Dynamic assessment when applicable, multimodal perfusion, echo and escalation of monitoring. Full 50-statement table not imported; graded statements differ from good-practice statements.
  2. S02 · Surviving Sepsis Campaign adult guideline, 2026. Official recommendations published 23 March 2026 checked. Initial MAP 65; a conditional 60–65 range for age65+; conditional initial crystalloid guidance with individualization; peripheral pressor evidence has unspecified universal limits. Local adoption required.
  3. S03 · ANDROMEDA-SHOCK, 2019. Primary abstract checked. CRT-targeted versus lactate-targeted septic shock protocol did not meet its primary 28-day mortality comparison. Adjunctive CRT does not become a single-marker treatment command.
  4. S04 · ANDROMEDA-SHOCK-2, 2025. Primary abstract checked. Early septic shock personalized CRT/phenotype protocol improved a hierarchical composite, mainly via duration of vital support. Do not describe this as proven isolated mortality benefit or copy trial protocol into an unvalidated bedside algorithm.
  5. S05 · FRESH fluid response evaluation trial, 2020. Primary abstract checked. Flow-based PLR-guided strategy had lower fluid balance in a relatively small trial. Method/platform/protocol and study limitations preclude universal outcome or every-device claims.
  6. S06 · CLOVERS, 2023. Primary abstract checked. Early sepsis-induced hypotension after initial fluids: restrictive/early-pressor versus liberal strategy did not significantly change the primary mortality endpoint. Not evidence that fluids or pressors are never indicated.
  7. S07 · CLASSIC, 2022. Primary abstract checked. ICU septic shock after initial fluid: restricted versus standard strategy had similar 90-day mortality. Phase and rescue conditions matter.
  8. S08 · ARISE FLUIDS, 2026. Primary abstract checked directly after connector limits. Published online 11 June 2026, after SSC2026 publication. Early ED septic shock: restrictive/early-pressor strategy did not improve days alive out of hospital at90days; pulmonary-edema findings do not establish universal superiority.
  9. S09 · SOAP II, 2010. Primary abstract checked. Dopamine versus norepinephrine in shock: more arrhythmic events with dopamine; overall primary mortality not significantly different. Cardiogenic subgroup results are not a universal all-agent comparison.
  10. S10 · DOREMI, 2021. Primary abstract checked. Small cardiogenic shock trial found no significant primary composite difference between milrinone and dobutamine. Not equivalence proof for all patients or a dosing protocol.
  11. S11 · ACC concise cardiogenic shock clinical guidance, 2025. Primary citation and official ACC guidance announcement checked. Early recognition, cause/perfusion/congestion assessment, team pathways and invasive hemodynamic profiling. This is expert guidance; not proof PAC insertion itself improves survival in every patient.
  12. S12 · SCAI shock-stage consensus update, 2022. Official society summary checked. Stage, etiology/phenotype and other risk modifiers are distinct; arrest modifiers and trajectories matter. Exact table requires local versioned curation before automated candidate staging.
  13. S13 · ECLS-SHOCK, 2023. Primary abstract checked. Early ECLS in infarct-related cardiogenic shock with planned revascularization did not reduce30-day mortality and increased bleeding/vascular complications. Not evidence against every carefully selected rescue use.
  14. S14 · DanGer-Shock, 2024. Primary publication checked directly. Selected STEMI cardiogenic-shock population: microaxial pump reduced180-day mortality with greater adverse events. Strict eligibility, device/center expertise and competing harms prevent all-shock extrapolation.
  15. S15 · SMART, 2018. Primary abstract checked. Pragmatic cluster-crossover ICU trial favored balanced fluid for a kidney/death composite. Composite, design, population and later trials limit universal mortality claims.
  16. S16 · PLUS, 2022. Primary abstract checked directly. ICU balanced multielectrolyte solution versus saline did not reduce primary mortality or AKI outcomes. Product and population specificity remain relevant.
  17. S17 · BaSICS, 2021. Primary abstract checked. Balanced solution versus saline did not significantly reduce90-day mortality. Do not imply every balanced-fluid product has identical benefit in every context.
