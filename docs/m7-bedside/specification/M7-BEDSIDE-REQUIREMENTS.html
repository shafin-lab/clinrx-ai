Hemodynamics: simple at the bedside,
explicit underneath

One focused review of pressure, perfusion, likely cause and the next clinical step. This specification strengthens the existing recorded-evidence M7; it does not replace its source contracts or convert its timing inference into a treatment engine.

Design status: Proposed requirements and test specifications, not a validated clinical implementation. All new clinical rules and numerical profiles are disabled. No application code, pump setting, clinical gate or hospital protocol was changed. Real-time patient use requires the source, profile, engineering and clinical acceptance evidence specified below.
1 · Pressure

Can I trust it?

Actual BP and method; this patient's target; source age and reliability. Missing target and unreliable measurement are explicit.

2 · Perfusion

Are the organs perfused?

Relevant bedside perfusion and lactate trajectory, with discordance and uncertainty. Pressure at target is not a global all-clear.

3 · Problem

What is the likely cause?

Clinician-assessed phenotype, including mixed or undifferentiated shock. Key echo/flow/rhythm context appears when relevant.

4 · Next step

What needs review now?

Short prioritized review options, responsible team and next reassessment. Decisions, orders and actual treatment remain distinct.

These cards describe the intended screen; they contain no patient data and do not represent a working clinical application.

1. Bedside simplicity is a requirement

Keep one primary review screen with the four cards above. Immediately below it show a short Review next area and one intervention/response timeline. The main view contains current clinically relevant information, not the full field dictionary. Reuse existing recorded data; request only a missing input that changes the current assessment or chosen action.

Always retain patient/encounter identity, review time, affected source freshness and the responsible team. Put the actual measurement time and method beside values. Use plain states: “Below documented target,” “Pressure unreliable,” “Perfusion concern,” “Not assessed,” “Source delayed” and “Specialist review needed.” Do not display “Stable,” “Covered,” or “Safe” based on one number, an empty feed or a completed software review.

  • Default view: relevant BP/MAP, HR/rhythm, documented pressure target, concise perfusion summary, actual vasoactive delivery, phenotype/uncertainty, and the next owned review. Show a lactate trend only if available and comparable; do not require lactate to open the screen.
  • Progressive disclosure: select a card to inspect its source evidence, contradictions and prerequisites. Advanced CO/PAC/echo details, fluid tests, concentration arithmetic and specialist/device context open only for the clinical question. One normal number cannot hide an outstanding safety concern.
  • Minimal entry: typically confirm assessment/target, select the relevant next review and record rationale/owner when missing. Do not ask users to manually enter hundreds of integration fields. An incomplete form never blocks the institution's urgent bedside response.
  • Action labels: “Confirm measurement,” “Review perfusion,” “Assess fluid benefit and harm,” “Review vasoactive delivery,” “Escalate to senior/shock team,” and “Record plan.” Only locally enabled options appear. The screen does not remotely start, stop or titrate treatment.
  • Timeline: actual administrations and measured responses at their real times, with gaps and confounding events. Ordered/planned interventions have a distinct style and cannot look delivered. No causal claim from a before/after picture alone.
  • Interaction: keyboard and screen-reader access, text with every color state, no color-only urgency, visible source age, appropriate decimal precision, safe loading/error/empty states, no hidden treatment defaults. Preserve selection during ordinary refresh; stale clinical proposals require explicit review.

The information inventory is broad because the software must handle uncertainty and safety accurately. It is a backend contract and disclosure model, not a bedside form or an instruction to order every possible investigation.

What is added to the existing module

Keep existing recorded evidence, source fidelity and protected timing review. Add clinician targets and phenotype, valid fluid-response/tolerance review, governed dose/delivery safety checks, accountable next steps and reassessment. No new clinical capability is active merely because this design exists.

What the clinician should never have to infer

  • Whether a value is measured, calculated, stale, unreliable or hypothetical.
  • Whether a medication or fluid was ordered, actually delivered or unknown.
  • Whether target pressure means perfusion is adequate.
  • Whether a review was acknowledged, a clinical decision made, or treatment actually performed.
  • Who is responsible for the next assessment and pending concern.
